Overview

Build something that matters. In three days.

Participants are placed into teams of three. Over three days, each team writes its own problem statement under one or more of the three #HACK26 themes, gets it approved, and builds a working Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that solves it.

On the final day, teams pitch to a judging panel and showcase at the exhibition.

Only two things are compulsory to submit: your approved problem statement and your MVP.

Everything else is optional.

Your Team

Three to a team, your pick or ours

3

Teams are strictly three members each.

If you already have people you'd like to team up with, let the Organising Committee know and you will be arranged into the same team of three.

No preference? No problem. The Organising Committee will allocate you into a team of three, balancing proficiency levels so that all teams start on a fair and even footing.

Team allocations will be announced by the Organising Committee. Teams cannot be swapped or re-arranged once announced.

Every member is expected to contribute. Whether all three present at the pitch is up to the team.

There is no minimum headcount to stay eligible. If a member is absent for part or all of the event, the team follows the programme as scheduled with the members present.

How You'll Build It

Two steps: write it, then build it

Step One

Craft and get your problem statement approved

Unlike hackathons where the problems are handed to you, at #HACK26 your team writes its own problem statement. This is the foundation of everything you build, so it carries real weight with the judges.

Day 1 is the only time set aside in the programme for brainstorming and crafting. Your problem statement must therefore be finalised on Day 1 (28 Sep).

Once finalised, approach the Organising Committee for approval straight away. There is no need to wait for a fixed checkpoint.

Approval unlocks your build. The moment your problem statement is approved, you may start working on your MVP — even if that is still on Day 1. You do not have to wait for Day 2.

The earlier you get approved, the more build time you have. There is no strict penalty for taking longer, but it is strongly encouraged that you finish and get approved by the end of Day 1. If your team is struggling, seek guidance from the facilitators rather than stalling.

A strong problem statement is:

01

Specific

It names who is affected and in what situation, not just a broad topic.

02

Real

It describes something that actually causes friction, delay or difficulty today.

03

Solvable in three days

Narrow enough that a small working prototype can meaningfully address it.

04

Clearly within a theme

Smarter, Gamifying or Well-Being RENO — combining themes is allowed.

Step Two

Build your MVP

A Minimum Viable Product is the smallest working version of your idea that actually demonstrates the solution. It does not need to be complete, polished or production-ready. It needs to work well enough for a judge to see the core concept in action.

Use any help you want

AI tools and vibe coding are not only permitted — they are encouraged. Use whatever assistants, generators or no-code tools help you move faster.

All forms of help are allowed. There is no restriction on outside references, tutorials, libraries, templates or open resources.

Facilitators are on site throughout. If your team is stuck — on the idea, the build or the pitch — consult them.

The one design rule to respect

Think beyond today’s constraints. Consider feasibility, scalability, sustainability and resources without limiting creativity.

Handling classified or sensitive information

This hackathon event is classified Restricted and below. If any part of your problem statement, MVP or underlying idea touches information that is classified Confidential and above, it is not allowed. When in doubt about the classification of anything you plan to use or show, ask the Organising Committee before proceeding.

What You Must Submit

Two things. That's it.

1

Your crafted problem statement

The approved problem statement your team wrote, stating the theme(s) it falls under, who is affected and what the problem is.

2

Your Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

The working prototype your team built to solve that problem statement.

"These two submissions are all that is required of your team. Nothing else is compulsory."

Optional submissions

Anything beyond the two items above — slides, written documentation, demo videos, source files, supporting material — is entirely optional.

How to submit

All submissions are made through a Google Drive link that will be provided by the Organising Committee. The link will be issued to all teams during the event.

Submit through the Google Drive link only — submissions sent through any other channel may not be picked up. If you hit any trouble submitting, flag it to a facilitator on site immediately.

How You Will Be Judged

Four criteria. 25 points each. 100 total.

All four carry equal weight. The judging panel's decision is final.

25

Solution Relevance & Impact

Problem fit, impact, value

25

Innovation & Creativity

Originality, uniqueness, wow factor

25

Prototype

Functionality, usability, viability

25

Pitch & Presentation

Clarity, confidence, storytelling

Full rubric

Judging rubric — full descriptors from 5 to 25 points across four criteria
Criteria 5 pts 10 pts 15 pts 20 pts 25 pts
Solution Relevance & Impact Solution is unclear or not relevant. Solution addresses the problem with limited relevance or benefit. Solution addresses the problem with moderate relevance or benefits. Solution addresses the problem with clear operational or workplace benefits. Solution addresses the problem with strong and practical benefits for the formation.
Innovation & Creativity Solution replicates existing ideas with little or no originality. Minor improvements to existing solutions. Solution demonstrates some creativity with meaningful improvements. Innovative solution with a creative application of technology or new ideas. Highly innovative and creative solution with clear value-add.
Prototype No prototype or unable to demonstrate the solution. Basic concept with limited functionality. Prototype demonstrates the core concept. Functional prototype with key features implemented. Well-developed prototype that clearly demonstrates the proposed solution.
Pitch & Presentation Unclear and difficult to understand. Basic presentation with limited explanation. Clear presentation covering the key points. Well-structured and convincing presentation with good responses to questions. Highly engaging, persuasive presentation with confident responses and a clear value proposition.

Ownership, Conduct and Questions

The fine print

Ownership of Your Work

All problem statements, prototypes and materials produced for #HACK26 are the property of the Singapore Armed Forces. By taking part, you accept that work created during the hackathon may be retained, showcased or developed further by the organisation.

Conduct

  • Work respectfully with your assigned teammates, including those from other units.
  • Be mindful of what information you bring into your solution and how you present it. Use good judgement about anything sensitive.
  • Keep to the timings in the programme, particularly on Day 3 where the schedule is tight.
  • Follow the directions of the Organising Committee and facilitators throughout the event.

Questions

For anything not covered here — including clarifications on your problem statement, your MVP or your submission — approach the facilitators on site.
